Kenya Health Surveillance Report 2024

Malaria in Kenya:
Malaria remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in Kenya, accounting for 30 percent of all outpatient visits. The lake endemic zone covering Nyanza and Western regions bears the highest burden. Counties including Kisumu, Homa Bay, and Migori report the highest incidence rates of 200 to 300 cases per 1000 population annually. The national malaria prevalence among children under five is 8 percent according to the 2023 Kenya Malaria Indicator Survey.

Tuberculosis in Kenya:
Kenya ranks among the 30 high TB burden countries globally. The national TB incidence rate is 292 per 100,000 population. Nairobi County accounts for the highest TB burden due to urban overcrowding and HIV co-infection. Treatment success rate stands at 87 percent against the WHO target of 90 percent.

HIV and AIDS:
Kenya has made significant progress in HIV response. Adult HIV prevalence stands at 4.2 percent. An estimated 1.4 million people are living with HIV. Antiretroviral therapy coverage has reached 84 percent. Mother to child transmission has been reduced to below 10 percent in most counties.

Maternal and Child Health:
Maternal mortality ratio stands at 362 per 100,000 live births. Skilled birth attendance has increased to 70 percent nationally. Under five mortality rate is 41 per 1000 live births. Stunting among children under five affects 26 percent of the population.

Non-Communicable Diseases:
Non-communicable diseases account for 27 percent of all deaths in Kenya. Hypertension affects 24 percent of adults. Diabetes prevalence stands at 3.3 percent. Cervical cancer is the most common cancer among women. Mental health disorders affect an estimated 25 percent of Kenyans at some point in their lives.
